Here's A Bunch Of Numbers

Every design review I have ever sat in ends the same way: someone asks whether the new version is better. Opinions fly, so I bring numbers. Not a dashboard of thirty charts, just the few that tell us whether people got what they came for.

A metric is only useful if it can change a decision on Monday morning.

Here are the five I track on almost every project:

  • Task success. Can a first-time visitor finish the job? Five people, one prototype, no hints.
  • Time on task. How long it takes, compared with the old flow.
  • Drop-off. The single step where the most people leave.
  • Error rate. Wrong fields, dead taps and back-button rescues.
  • Support questions. The cheapest research you already own.

None of these needs a big budget. A quiet afternoon with five testers and a stopwatch covers the first three, and your inbox covers the last.

When the numbers move the wrong way I get curious rather than defensive. A falling success rate usually points at one confusing label, and a one-word fix is the best kind of redesign.
